Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript **"Provenance-aware, risk-adaptive mediation of AI-agent tool calls against indirect prompt injection"** for consideration as a full-length research article in *Computers & Security*.

**Why this journal.** Tool-using AI agents now read e-mail, documents and web pages and act on their users' behalf. Indirect prompt injection turns that combination into a practical attack, and deployed agents increasingly rely on the human, through approval dialogs, as the last line of defence. The manuscript addresses this at the intersection of system security and the human aspects of security that the journal and IFIP TC11 cover.

**Contributions.**
1. CLEAR, a mediator that attributes every argument of a proposed tool call to its source, scores contextual risk from interpretable features and escalates through four tiers. We prove a conditional escalation floor that attacker-controlled text cannot lower, and extend it to values laundered through encodings.
2. An environment-grounded evaluation that replays 24,648 recorded trajectories of 22 LLM agents and five published defences in AgentDojo, recomputing the benchmark's own predicates. On held-out data, CLEAR reduces attack success from 16.5% to 0.69% with an ideal user while asking 51% fewer questions than confirming every action. The protection survives detector-evading phrasing and value laundering. Statistics are clustered by benchmark task.
3. A cross-benchmark stress test against two alternative engines, run blind to the labels, and a habituation-aware user model that is clearly labelled as simulation.
4. A complete, openly released laboratory study apparatus, protocol and simulation-based power analysis for the human-subject evaluation that should follow.

**Transparency.** No human participants were involved; the user study is a proposed protocol and has not been run. Every result about people comes from an explicitly labelled simulation. All code, data and analysis scripts are publicly available (https://github.com/mazharulmd/HCI), and every number in the paper is generated by the released code. The use of generative AI tools in the research and in drafting is disclosed in the manuscript, as Elsevier's policy requires.

This manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The authors declare no competing interests.
